# Decision Frameworks

## When to use
When the engagement has a pending decision with multiple options or stakeholders, or when decisions keep getting re-litigated.

## Choosing a framework

| Situation | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Many stakeholders, ambiguous accountability | RAPID | Forces explicit roles |
| Cross-functional decision, need clarity | DACI | Lighter than RAPID |
| Choosing among scored options | Weighted scoring | Quantitative, defensible |
| Prioritizing a backlog | Impact / Effort 2x2 | Fast, visual |
| Broad backlog with categories | MoSCoW | Categorical, easy to communicate |
| Strategic bets | Three Horizons | Time-horizon framing |
| Pre-decision, unfreeze status quo | Pre-mortem | Surfaces risks upfront |

## RAPID

Roles per decision:
- **R**ecommend: Drives the analysis and proposes the answer
- **A**gree: Must sign off (veto right) — keep this list short
- **P**erform: Will execute the decision
- **I**nput: Provides expertise; no veto
- **D**ecide: Makes the call

Document one-page per major decision: who plays each role, decision date, decision summary.

## DACI (lighter alternative)

- **D**river: Owns the decision process (not the decision itself)
- **A**pprover: Has final say
- **C**ontributors: Provide info / opinions
- **I**nformed: Need to know after

DACI works when accountability is clearer than in RAPID-territory.

## Weighted scoring

When choosing among 3+ options on multiple criteria:

1. List criteria; assign weights (must sum to 100)
2. Score each option 1-5 on each criterion
3. Multiply, sum, rank
4. Sensitivity: re-weight criteria ±20% — does ranking change?

Surface scoring transparently — the matrix matters more than the number. If results feel wrong, the criteria are wrong, not the math.

## Impact / Effort 2x2

| | Low impact | High impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Low effort** | Easy wins (do quickly) | Quick wins (do first) |
| **High effort** | Avoid (don't do) | Major projects (plan carefully) |

Place candidate initiatives, then sequence: Quick wins → Major projects → Easy wins → cull Avoid.

## MoSCoW

- **Must have**: Failure if missing
- **Should have**: Important but workaround exists
- **Could have**: Desired if time/budget allow
- **Won't have (this time)**: Explicitly out of scope

Use for backlog prioritization with execs who don't want to count points.

## Three Horizons (strategic bets)

- **H1** (defend & extend core): Months
- **H2** (emerging opportunities): 1-3 years
- **H3** (long-term bets): 3-5+ years

Useful when the conversation is "should we do this OR that?" — answer is often "both, sequenced".

## Pre-mortem

Before committing to a major decision, imagine it's 12 months from now and the initiative failed. Brainstorm causes. Categorize: external / internal / technical / political / market. Surface mitigations.

Catches risks the team would otherwise hand-wave away.

## Quality bar

- Every major decision has a documented framework + roles
- Decisions are stored in the engagement brain (not just slack history)
- Re-litigation triggers a refresh of the framework artifact, not a new debate
- The framework is shared with the client for transparency

## Common failure modes

- Using RAPID for trivial decisions (overkill)
- Weighted scoring with too many criteria (>7 makes everything average)
- Skipping the sensitivity check (false precision)
- Pre-mortem treated as a "concerns" list without mitigations
