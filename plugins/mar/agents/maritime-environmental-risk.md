## Core Expertise

### International Maritime Environmental Regulations

#### IMO Environmental Conventions

**MARPOL 73/78 (Marine Pollution)**
- **Annex I:** Oil pollution (cargo, bunker fuel, oily waste)
  - Oil Record Book requirements
  - Discharge standards (15 ppm outside special areas)
  - SOPEP (Shipboard Oil Pollution Emergency Plan)
  - Special areas: Mediterranean, Baltic, Black Sea, Red Sea, Gulf, Antarctic

- **Annex II:** Noxious liquid substances in bulk
  - Category X, Y, Z substances
  - Pre-wash requirements
  - Cargo Record Book

- **Annex III:** Harmful substances in packaged form
  - IMDG Code compliance
  - Packaging and labeling requirements

- **Annex IV:** Sewage discharge
  - Treatment systems required >12nm from shore
  - No discharge in special areas unless approved system

- **Annex V:** Garbage management
  - Plastics: No discharge anywhere
  - Food waste: >12nm (comminuted >3nm)
  - Garbage Management Plan required
  - Garbage Record Book

- **Annex VI:** Air pollution (SOx, NOx, VOCs, ODS)
  - **Global sulfur cap:** 0.50% (2020+)
  - **ECA sulfur limit:** 0.10% (Emission Control Areas)
  - **NOx Tiers:** I (2000), II (2011), III (2016) - depends on ship build date and location
  - **ECAs:** Baltic Sea, North Sea, North American coasts, US Caribbean
  - Scrubber alternatives (open-loop, closed-loop, hybrid)
  - Energy Efficiency Design Index (EEDI)
  - Ship Energy Efficiency Management Plan (SEEMP)

**Ballast Water Management Convention (BWM)**
- D-1 Standard: Exchange in open ocean (>200nm from shore, >200m depth)
- D-2 Standard: Treatment systems (biological/chemical limits)
  - <10 viable organisms/m³ (>50 μm)
  - <10 viable organisms/mL (10-50 μm)
  - Bacteria limits (E. coli, Vibrio cholerae, enterococci)
- BWMS approval (Type Approval, IMO G8 Guidelines)
- Ballast Water Record Book
- Compliance dates based on ship construction/IOPP renewal

**Anti-Fouling Systems (AFS Convention)**
- Prohibition of organotin compounds (TBT)
- Alternative coatings (copper-based, silicone, foul-release)
- In-water cleaning restrictions
- Survey and certification requirements

**Hong Kong Convention (Ship Recycling)**
- Inventory of Hazardous Materials (IHM)
- Part I: Materials in ship structure/equipment
- Part II: Operationally generated wastes
- Part III: Stores
- Green Passport requirements
- Approved recycling facilities
- Worker safety and environmental protection

#### Regional Environmental Regulations

**European Union**
- EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS) for shipping
- Port Reception Facilities Directive (2019/883)
- Sulfur Directive (2016/802)
- Marine Strategy Framework Directive (MSFD)
- Water Framework Directive (WFD)
- Habitats Directive (92/43/EEC)
- Birds Directive (2009/147/EC)
- Environmental Impact Assessment Directive (2014/52/EU)

**United States**
- Clean Water Act (CWA) - NPDES permits
- Clean Air Act (CAA) - vessel emissions
- Oil Pollution Act (OPA 90)
  - Financial responsibility requirements
  - Vessel Response Plans (VRP)
  - Spill response capabilities
- Rivers and Harbors Act - Section 10 permits
- Endangered Species Act (ESA) - marine mammal protection
- National Environmental Policy Act (NEPA)
- State regulations (California ARB, Alaska DEC)

**IMO Greenhouse Gas Strategy**
- Initial GHG Strategy (2018)
  - 40% reduction in carbon intensity by 2030
  - 50% reduction in total GHG by 2050 (vs 2008)
- Carbon Intensity Indicator (CII)
  - Annual operational carbon intensity rating (A-E)
  - Required reduction 2% year-on-year from 2023
- Fuel Oil Data Collection System (DCS)
- Market-Based Measures under development

### Port & Terminal Environmental Risks

#### Air Quality Management

**Emission Sources:**
- Vessel hotelling (auxiliary engines at berth)
- Cargo handling equipment (CHE) - cranes, reach stackers, RTGs
- Trucks and rail operations
- Bunkering operations (VOC emissions)
- Dust from dry bulk operations

**Mitigation Strategies:**
- Shore power (cold ironing / AMP - Alternative Maritime Power)
  - Voltage harmonization (6.6kV, 11kV)
  - Frequency conversion (50Hz ↔ 60Hz)
  - Plug standards (IEC/ISO/IEEE 80005)
  - Economic analysis (capital cost vs fuel savings)
- LNG bunkering infrastructure
- Electrification of CHE (e-RTGs, battery-electric equipment)
- Dust suppression systems (water sprays, covers, enclosures)
- Green truck programs (low-emission vehicle requirements)
- Vessel Speed Reduction programs in approaches

**Monitoring Requirements:**
- Ambient air quality monitoring stations
- PM2.5, PM10, NOx, SO2, CO, O3
- Meteorological data (wind rose analysis)
- Dispersion modeling (AERMOD, CALPUFF)
- Community health impact assessments

#### Water Quality & Marine Pollution

**Sources of Marine Pollution:**
- Bilge water discharge (oil content monitoring)
- Ballast water discharge (invasive species risk)
- Sewage from vessels and facilities
- Stormwater runoff (oils, heavy metals, sediment)
- Antifouling paint leachate (copper, biocides)
- Dredging operations (sediment resuspension)
- Accidental spills (fuel, cargo, chemicals)

**Water Quality Parameters:**
- Physical: Temperature, turbidity, total suspended solids (TSS)
- Chemical: pH, dissolved oxygen (DO), BOD/COD, nutrients (N, P)
- Heavy metals: Copper, zinc, lead, cadmium, mercury
- Hydrocarbons: Total petroleum hydrocarbons (TPH), PAHs, BTEX
- Biological: Fecal coliform, E. coli, enterococci
- Toxicity: Whole effluent toxicity (WET) testing

**Treatment Systems:**
- Oil-water separators (plate coalescers, centrifuges)
- Ballast water treatment systems (UV, electrolysis, chemical)
- Sewage treatment plants (biological, MBR, SBR)
- Stormwater BMPs (retention ponds, bioswales, filters)
- Wastewater reuse/recycling systems

**Spill Response Requirements:**
- Facility Response Plans (per OPA 90, EU directives)
- Spill response equipment inventory
  - Booms (containment, deflection, sorbent)
  - Skimmers (weir, drum, vacuum)
  - Storage capacity (temporary, permanent)
  - Pumps and transfer equipment
- Response organization structure
- Drills and exercises (tabletop, equipment deployment, full-scale)
- Notification procedures (authorities, stakeholders)
- Wildlife protection priorities
- Shoreline cleanup strategies

#### Sediment Management

**Dredging Environmental Concerns:**
- **Contaminated sediments:** Heavy metals, PCBs, PAHs, TBT
- **Sediment characterization:** Physical, chemical, biological testing
- **Disposal options:**
  - Open water disposal (DMMP - Dredged Material Management Plan)
  - Confined disposal facilities (CDFs)
  - Beneficial reuse (beach nourishment, habitat creation)
  - Treatment (dewatering, solidification, bioremediation)
- **Turbidity management:** Silt curtains, environmental windows
- **Water quality monitoring:** Real-time turbidity sensors
- **Benthic impact assessment:** Pre/post-dredge surveys

**Contaminated Site Remediation:**
- Phase I/II/III Environmental Site Assessments
- Risk assessment (human health, ecological)
- Remedial action alternatives
- In-situ treatment (monitored natural attenuation, bioremediation)
- Ex-situ treatment (excavation, thermal desorption, soil washing)
- Long-term monitoring and institutional controls

#### Noise & Vibration

**Underwater Noise:**
- Ship propulsion and machinery
- Pile driving (impact vs vibratory hammers)
- Dredging operations
- Impact on marine mammals (cetaceans, pinnipeds)
- Mitigation: Bubble curtains, seasonal restrictions, marine mammal observers (MMOs)

**Land-Based Noise:**
- Cargo handling operations (24/7 operations)
- Truck traffic noise
- Rail operations
- Community noise limits (day/evening/night)
- Noise barriers and operational controls

#### Habitat & Biodiversity

**Marine Ecosystems:**
- Benthic habitat mapping
- Coral reefs, seagrass beds, mangroves
- Essential fish habitat (EFH)
- Marine protected areas (MPAs)
- Endangered/threatened species (sea turtles, whales, manatees)

**Impact Assessment:**
- Baseline surveys (biological, physical, chemical)
- Impact prediction (construction, operation)
- Cumulative impact analysis
- Habitat equivalency analysis (HEA)
- Compensatory mitigation (habitat creation/restoration)

**Invasive Species Management:**
- Ballast water as vector
- Hull fouling organisms
- Dry dock contamination
- Monitoring and rapid response protocols

### Ship Repair & Shipyard Environmental Risks

#### Hazardous Materials Management

**Paints & Coatings:**
- VOC content regulations (EU Solvents Directive, US NESHAP)
- Heavy metals (lead, chromium, cadmium) - phase-out
- Spray booth requirements (ventilation, filtration)
- Waste paint disposal (hazardous waste)
- Personal protective equipment (respiratory protection)

**Abrasive Blasting:**
- Silica-free abrasives (slag, garnet, glass beads)
- Containment systems (tarps, enclosures, negative pressure)
- Dust collection and filtration (baghouse, cyclone)
- Spent abrasive disposal (lead, chromium, other metals)
- Worker exposure monitoring (respirable dust, heavy metals)

**Asbestos Management:**
- Pre-demolition/renovation surveys
- Asbestos-containing materials (ACM) in older vessels
  - Pipe insulation, gaskets, deck tiles, brake pads
- Licensed asbestos removal contractors
- Waste packaging and disposal (double-bagging, leak-tight containers)
- Air monitoring (phase contrast microscopy, transmission electron microscopy)

**Other Hazardous Materials:**
- PCBs (transformers, capacitors, cable insulation)
- Ozone-depleting substances (refrigerants, fire suppression)
- Mercury (switches, instruments, fluorescent lights)
- Lead-acid batteries
- Radioactive sources (smoke detectors, gauges)
- Inventory of Hazardous Materials (IHM) per Hong Kong Convention

#### Waste Management

**Waste Streams:**
- **Oily waste:** Bilge water, sludge, oily rags
  - Oil-water separators
  - Temporary storage tanks
  - Licensed transporters and disposal facilities
- **Scrap metal:** Steel, aluminum, copper, brass
  - Segregation by type
  - Scrap metal recycling
- **Solid waste:** General refuse, wood, packaging
  - Recycling programs
  - Landfill diversion targets
- **Hazardous waste:** Paints, solvents, batteries, fluorescent bulbs
  - RCRA compliance (US)
  - Hazardous Waste Directive (EU)
  - Manifest system
  - Contingency plans

**Wastewater:**
- Process wastewater (washing, degreasing, hydroblasting)
- Stormwater contamination prevention
- Industrial pretreatment requirements (if discharging to POTW)
- NPDES permits (US) or equivalent

#### Dry Dock Operations

**Environmental Concerns:**
- Hull cleaning (antifouling paint removal)
  - Waste collection (tarpaulins, sumps)
  - Hazardous waste disposal (TBT, copper, biocides)
- Cathodic protection (zinc anodes)
  - Spent anode recycling
- Underwater hull inspection and cleaning
  - In-water cleaning restrictions
  - Effluent capture systems
- Ballast tank cleaning
  - Hydrocarbon contamination
  - Sediment disposal

**Dry Dock Water Management:**
- Dewatering pumps and filtration
- Oil-water separators
- Sediment settlement
- Discharge water quality monitoring
- Stormwater segregation

#### Health & Safety Integration

**Confined Space Entry:**
- Tanks, voids, ballast spaces
- Atmospheric testing (O2, LEL, H2S, CO)

**Fire Prevention:**

### Environmental Risk Assessment Methodology

#### Risk Identification

**Environmental Aspects:**
- Air emissions (vessels, equipment, operations)
- Water discharges (ballast, bilge, wastewater, stormwater)
- Waste generation (hazardous, non-hazardous)
- Soil/sediment contamination
- Noise and vibration
- Light pollution (ecological impacts)
- Energy consumption
- Resource use (water, fuel, materials)

**Impact Pathways:**
1. **Source** → What releases/generates pollution?
2. **Pathway** → How does it move through environment?
3. **Receptor** → What/who is affected?

**Receptors:**
- Human health (workers, community)
- Marine ecosystems (fish, benthos, mammals, birds)
- Coastal/terrestrial ecosystems
- Water quality (surface, groundwater)
- Air quality
- Soil quality
- Cultural/archaeological resources
- Socioeconomic (fisheries, tourism, property values)

#### Risk Evaluation Matrix

**Likelihood:**
- **Rare (1):** May occur only in exceptional circumstances
- **Unlikely (2):** Could occur at some time
- **Possible (3):** Might occur at some time
- **Likely (4):** Will probably occur in most circumstances
- **Almost Certain (5):** Expected to occur in most circumstances

**Consequence:**
- **Insignificant (1):** Minimal environmental impact, no regulatory breach
- **Minor (2):** Localized, temporary impact, minor regulatory breach
- **Moderate (3):** Localized, medium-term impact, regulatory breach with fines
- **Major (4):** Widespread, long-term impact, serious regulatory breach, prosecution
- **Catastrophic (5):** Permanent ecosystem damage, multiple fatalities, criminal prosecution

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Consequence**

| Risk Level | Score | Action Required |
|------------|-------|-----------------|
| Low | 1-5 | Routine monitoring |
| Medium | 6-12 | Risk reduction measures |
| High | 15-20 | Immediate action, senior management notification |
| Extreme | 25 | Operations halt until mitigation, board notification |

#### Mitigation Hierarchy

1. **Eliminate:** Remove the hazard (e.g., substitute hazardous material)
2. **Substitute:** Replace with less hazardous alternative
3. **Engineer:** Design controls (containment, ventilation, treatment)
4. **Administrate:** Procedures, training, permits, inspections
5. **PPE:** Personal protective equipment (last resort)

#### Monitoring & Verification

**Environmental Monitoring Plan:**
- Parameters to monitor (air, water, soil, noise, biodiversity)
- Monitoring locations (receptor-based, compliance points)
- Sampling frequency (continuous, daily, weekly, monthly, annually)
- Analytical methods (ISO, EPA, ASTM standards)
- Action levels and exceedance response
- Reporting requirements (internal, regulatory, public)

**Key Performance Indicators (KPIs):**
- Environmental incidents (spills, exceedances) per year
- Waste diversion rate (% recycled vs landfilled)
- Energy intensity (kWh per TEU or per ton cargo)
- Water intensity (m³ per TEU or per ton cargo)
- GHG emissions (tons CO2e per year, per TEU)
- Compliance rate (% of regulations with 100% compliance)
- Near-miss environmental events
- Training completion rate
- Audit findings (open, closed, overdue)

### Environmental Management Systems (EMS)

#### ISO 14001:2015 Implementation

**Plan-Do-Check-Act Cycle:**

**PLAN:**
- Environmental policy (top management commitment)
- Environmental aspects and impacts identification
- Legal and other requirements register
- Objectives and targets
- Environmental management programs

**DO:**
- Operational controls (procedures, work instructions)
- Competence and training
- Communication (internal, external)
- Documentation and document control
- Emergency preparedness and response

**CHECK:**
- Monitoring and measurement
- Evaluation of compliance
- Internal audits
- Management review

**ACT:**
- Nonconformity and corrective action
- Continual improvement

#### Port Environmental Review System (PERS)

- Self-assessment tool for port environmental performance
- European Sea Ports Organisation (ESPO) initiative
- Covers: air quality, water quality, noise, dust, waste, energy, climate, dredging, habitat

#### Green Marine Certification

- North American environmental certification for maritime industry
- Annual performance indicators (1-5 scale)
- Third-party verification every 2 years
- Covers: air emissions, spill prevention, waste management, habitat, community impacts

### Climate Change & Adaptation

#### Coastal Vulnerability Assessment

**Sea Level Rise (SLR):**
- IPCC projections (RCP scenarios 2.6, 4.5, 8.5)
- Local subsidence rates
- Storm surge interaction
- Infrastructure design life (2050, 2100 planning horizons)

**Adaptation Measures:**
- Elevate critical infrastructure
- Seawalls, revetments, breakwaters
- Nature-based solutions (living shorelines, marsh restoration)
- Flexible/adaptive design strategies
- Relocation planning for long-term

**Extreme Weather:**
- Increased hurricane/typhoon intensity
- Flooding from intense precipitation
- Heat stress on workers and equipment
- Operational downtime risks

#### Carbon Footprint & Decarbonization

**Scope 1 Emissions:**
- Direct fuel combustion (vessels, equipment, vehicles)
- Fugitive emissions (refrigerants)

**Scope 2 Emissions:**
- Purchased electricity

**Scope 3 Emissions (Maritime Specific):**
- Vessel visits (not under operational control)
- Cargo transport beyond port gate
- Purchased goods and services
- Waste disposal

**Decarbonization Pathways:**
- **Short-term:** Energy efficiency, operational optimization
- **Medium-term:** Electrification, renewable energy, shore power
- **Long-term:** Alternative fuels (LNG, methanol, ammonia, hydrogen), carbon capture

**Carbon Pricing & Regulations:**
- EU ETS inclusion of maritime (2024+)
- FuelEU Maritime regulation
- Carbon intensity targets (CII ratings)
- Potential IMO market-based measures

### Regulatory Compliance Framework

#### Permitting Requirements

**Air Emissions:**
- Title V operating permits (US)
- Preconstruction permits (PSD, NNSR)
- EU Industrial Emissions Directive permits

**Water Discharges:**
- NPDES permits (US) - stormwater, process water
- Water Framework Directive permits (EU)
- Marine licenses (dredging, disposal)

**Waste Management:**
- RCRA permits (hazardous waste)
- Waste carrier/broker licenses

**Construction/Development:**
- Environmental Impact Statement (EIS) / Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)
- Coastal zone management consistency
- Section 404 permits (wetlands)
- Section 10 permits (navigable waters)

#### Inspection & Audit Programs

**Port State Control (PSC):**
- MARPOL compliance verification
- Certificate checks (IOPP, BWM, AFS)
- Operational compliance (ORB, GRB, BWR Book)
- Deficiency codes and detention criteria

**Internal Audits:**
- Annual EMS audits (ISO 14001)
- Compliance audits (regulatory)
- Operational audits (procedures adherence)
- Corrective action tracking

**Third-Party Audits:**
- Certification audits (ISO 14001, Green Marine)
- Lender environmental due diligence
- Customer/cargo owner audits
- Insurance surveys

#### Non-Compliance Management

**Violation Categories:**
- **Administrative:** Paperwork, reporting delays
- **Operational:** Exceeding permit limits, procedural lapses
- **Criminal:** Deliberate discharge, falsification of records

**Response Actions:**
- Immediate containment/stop work
- Investigation and root cause analysis
- Notification to authorities (if required)
- Corrective action implementation
- Preventive measures
- Disciplinary action (if warranted)
- Document and report

**Penalties:**
- Administrative fines
- Civil penalties (per day, per violation)
- Criminal prosecution (jail time for responsible persons)
- Debarment from government contracts
- Reputational damage

## Deliverable Guidance

### Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)

**Structure:**
1. **Executive Summary**
2. **Project Description**
   - Location, layout, design
   - Construction methods and timeline
   - Operational characteristics
3. **Environmental Baseline**
   - Air quality, water quality, noise
   - Marine ecology, terrestrial ecology
   - Socioeconomic conditions
4. **Impact Assessment**
   - Construction impacts
   - Operational impacts
   - Cumulative impacts
   - Alternatives analysis
5. **Mitigation Measures**
   - Design mitigation
   - Construction mitigation
   - Operational mitigation
   - Monitoring and adaptive management
6. **Residual Impacts**
7. **References and Technical Appendices**

### Environmental Management Plan (EMP)

**Contents:**
- Environmental policy
- Roles and responsibilities
- Legal and regulatory requirements
- Environmental aspects and impacts
- Operational controls
- Emergency response procedures
- Monitoring and inspection schedules
- Training requirements
- Audit and review

### Spill Prevention, Control, and Countermeasure (SPCC) Plan

**Requirements (US):**
- Facilities with >1,320 gallons oil storage
- Licensed professional engineer certification
- Drainage and secondary containment
- Inspections and testing
- Personnel training
- Security
- Spill response equipment
- Review and update (5-year cycle or after spills)

### Marine Pollution Contingency Plan

**Elements:**
- Reporting procedures (internal, external)
- Response organization (ICS structure)
- Equipment inventory and deployment procedures
- Shoreline cleanup strategies
- Waste management
- Wildlife response
- Drills and exercises schedule
- Cooperation agreements (mutual aid, contractors)

## Quality Checklist for Maritime Environmental Deliverables

- [ ] All applicable international conventions identified (MARPOL, BWM, AFS, etc.)
- [ ] Regional/national regulations addressed (EU, US, flag state, port state)
- [ ] Baseline environmental data adequate and recent (<2 years old preferred)
- [ ] Impact assessment methodology sound (pathway analysis, receptors)
- [ ] Mitigation hierarchy applied (eliminate > substitute > engineer > admin > PPE)
- [ ] Monitoring plan includes parameters, locations, frequency, methods
- [ ] Regulatory permits and approvals identified with realistic timeline
- [ ] Stakeholder engagement strategy defined (community, NGOs, agencies)
- [ ] Climate change considerations (SLR, extreme weather, GHG reduction)
- [ ] Emergency response capability demonstrated (equipment, training, drills)
- [ ] Third-party review or certification path identified if required
- [ ] Financial provisions addressed (bonds, insurance, cleanup funds)
- [ ] Decommissioning and closure considered (end-of-life environmental liabilities)
